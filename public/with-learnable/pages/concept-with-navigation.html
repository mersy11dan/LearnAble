The Future of the VA Industry


The Virtual Assistant profession is not shrinking. It is evolving. As businesses become more digital, more global, and more comfortable with remote collaboration, the need for skilled support professionals continues to grow. At the same time, new technologies like Artificial Intelligence are changing how support work gets done and what clients will value most in the years ahead.

⚠️ An Important Distinction


Virtual Assistant can mean two different things. In some reports, a “virtual assistant” refers to AI software assistants such as chatbots, voice assistants, and digital agents. In other contexts, it refers to human remote professionals who provide business support online. The future of the industry includes both, and increasingly, they will work together.

What is Shaping the Future of VA Work?

  1. More remote-first businesses

Companies are now more comfortable building distributed teams, hiring remotely, and working across locations and time zones. That makes remote support roles more normal and more scalable.

  1. Growing demand for flexibility

Many businesses do not want to hire full-time, in-office staff for every task. They increasingly value support that is flexible, efficient, and easy to scale up or down.

  1. More digital operations

As businesses move more of their work online, they need people who can manage digital tools, communication systems, content workflows, and online coordination smoothly.

  1. Rising specialization

General support will still matter, but more clients are looking for VAs with niche strengths in areas like customer support, e-commerce, project coordination, social media, tech tools, and operations.

  1. AI-powered productivity

Artificial Intelligence is making it easier to draft, summarize, organize, research, schedule, and automate routine tasks. This will not remove the need for human VAs, but it will change what strong VA work looks like.

  1. Greater value placed on human skills

As automation handles more routine work, qualities like judgment, reliability, communication, empathy, problem-solving, and client care become even more valuable.

Artificial Intelligence and the Future of VA Work

Artificial Intelligence is one of the most important forces shaping the future of the Virtual Assistant profession. Many modern workplaces already use AI tools to help draft, summarize, research, organize information, and automate repetitive tasks.

This means that the future Virtual Assistant will not simply be someone who performs tasks manually. Increasingly, strong VAs will be those who know how to use AI tools effectively to improve productivity and quality of work.

How AI tools can support Virtual Assistants

AI systems can assist with many routine or time-consuming tasks, including:

  • Drafting emails, notes, and first-pass documents
  • Summarizing meetings or long reports
  • Researching information quickly
  • Organizing tasks, reminders, and workflows
  • Supporting scheduling and transcription
  • Handling simple customer queries through chatbots

Where human Virtual Assistants still add the most value

Even as AI tools improve, there are many areas where human VAs remain essential:

  • Managing client relationships with care and professionalism
  • Using judgment when priorities change or instructions are unclear
  • Communicating with empathy and emotional intelligence
  • Handling complex or sensitive requests
  • Coordinating teams, people, and moving parts across projects
  • Improving systems rather than simply following instructions

What Clients Are Likely to Value More and More

As the profession evolves, the most valuable VAs will likely be those who combine tool fluency with human strength.

AI fluency

Clients will increasingly value VAs who can use AI tools responsibly to save time, improve output quality, and streamline repetitive work.

Specialized expertise

The future will likely reward VAs who go beyond general support and develop deeper skills in one or two service areas.

Excellent human service

Responsiveness, dependability, emotional intelligence, professionalism, and clear communication will remain major differentiators.

What This Means for Aspiring Virtual Assistants

The future of this profession is encouraging, but it does call for growth. A strong future-ready VA will likely need to:

  • Stay comfortable learning new digital tools
  • Understand how to use AI as a support tool, not a shortcut for poor-quality work
  • Build strong communication and relationship skills
  • Develop a niche or service specialization over time
  • Work with adaptability, curiosity, and a problem-solving mindset

An encouraging truth


You do not need to master everything immediately. What matters is building a strong foundation, staying teachable, and growing with the industry as it changes.

So, is the Future of VA Promising?

Yes, but not in a passive way. The profession appears to be moving toward a future where Virtual Assistants who are adaptable, tech-aware, organized, specialized, and deeply human in how they serve will continue to be valuable. The future VA is likely to be more than an administrative helper. They may become a workflow partner, a client support specialist, an operations coordinator, a digital organizer, or an AI-enabled right hand for a business.

Rive Animations

The recommended way to embed a Rive animation is the Library Files tool () in the editor toolbar: pick a .riv asset, choose its artboard and data bindings, and insert it. This produces a library-anchored ::asset ID ... :: tag whose overrides are independent on each page — double-click an inserted asset to reopen the configurator. The raw ::rive data: form below is a low-level escape hatch.

Per-page overrides. The same library asset can render a different artboard, state machine or data bindings on each page. The overrides are configured in the Library Files tool and stored on the tag as a Base64 data: payload (::asset 42 data:<base64-json> ::) — you do not construct this by hand; double-click an inserted asset to reopen the configurator. The src, dimensions, brand and version still resolve from the library file, so the asset stays anchored. 

::rive data: <base64-encoded-JSON> ::

JSON shape:

{
  "src": "https://example.com/animation.riv",
  "width": 1920,
  "height": 540,
  "stateMachine": "State Machine 1",
  "artboard": "MasterBanner",
  "viewModelName": "mbViewModel",
  "viewModelInstanceName": "Instance",
  "bindings": {
    "bannerTitle": "Introduction to the Learning Content",
    "courseSelect": "artboard:SAMPLE_ARTBOARD"
  }
}

Fields:

  • src — URL to the hosted Rive file (.riv) — required
  • width, height — canvas size in pixels (defaults: 400×225)
  • stateMachine — name of the state machine to play
  • artboard — name of the artboard to render (uses default if omitted)
  • viewModelName, viewModelInstanceName — data binding View Model and instance names
  • bindings — object of { propertyName: value } pairs. Type (string, number, boolean, color, enum) is auto-detected. Use "artboard:<name>" as the value to bind an artboard property (e.g., to swap branded variants).

Dynamic values. Binding values may embed {{key}} placeholders, replaced per learner at render time (e.g. "Hello {{learner.first_name}}"). Available keys: learner.first_name, learner.last_name, learner.full_name, learner.email, course, brand, page_name, locale.

GitHub Content

GitHub content embeds pull a file from a repository's default branch at render time and display it inline. Use the GitHub insert tool in the editor toolbar to fill in the fields and validate the paths — you do not need to construct this by hand. 

::github-content data: <base64-encoded-JSON> ::

JSON shape:

{
  "org": "<org>",
  "repo": "<repo>",
  "path": "<path/to/file.md>",
  "files": ["<path/to/file1.py>", "<path/to/file2.py>"]
}

Fields:

  • org — GitHub organization or user — required
  • repo — repository name — required
  • path — path to the main content file, relative to the repository root — required
  • files — array of supporting file paths, rendered as download links above the main content. Each path is relative to the repository root (same format as path).

Markdown files (.md, .markdown) are rendered inline; other UTF-8 text files are shown in a code block, while non-UTF-8/binary files show a warning instead. Fetched content is cached for 1 hour.

Chidi InlineChat

Chidi embeds use a single data: attribute holding a Base64-encoded JSON config. Use the Chidi insert tool () in the editor toolbar to generate it; double-click an existing tag to edit its config in place — you do not need to construct this by hand. 

::chidi data: <base64-encoded-JSON> ::

JSON shape:

{
  "activityType": "tutor",
  "apiUrl": "https://example.com",
  "cssUrl": "https://example.com/inline/llm-tutor-inline.css",
  "greeting": "Hello! I'm here to help.",
  "jsUrl": "https://example.com/inline/llm-tutor-inline.js",
  "starters": ["I'm ready", "Check my code"],
  "systemPrompt": "You are a helpful tutor..."
}

Fields:

  • jsUrl, cssUrl, apiUrl — URLs for the InlineChat assets and API — required
  • greeting — initial greeting displayed to the student — required
  • starters — array of suggested starter prompts (optional)
  • systemPrompt — system prompt for the AI tutor — required
  • activityType — one of tutor, reflection, guide, content_clarity
